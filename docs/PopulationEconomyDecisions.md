# Open Fufu — Population and FFY Mechanics Decisions

## Status and precedence

This document records concrete decisions made after `docs/GameMechanics.md` while the population/front-allocation model is still being designed.

Where this document makes a concrete decision that `GameMechanics.md` still describes as unresolved or tentative, **this newer document takes precedence**. It should be consolidated back into the main mechanics document once the current mechanics discussion stabilizes.

This is a game-design document, not an implementation plan.

---

## 1. Population commitment is literal total-population commitment

When a controller commits a percentage of population, that percentage refers directly to the faction's **total current population**.

Example:

```text
Total population: 100,000
Commitment to Front A: 55%

=> desired commitment associated with Front A: 55,000 population
```

Do not introduce a hidden mobilizable-population fraction, civilian/military split, secret deployment ceiling, or other V1 exception that makes `55%` mean something other than 55% of current total population.

Open Fufu should prefer transparent mechanics over hidden modifiers and gotchas.

Population remains one core resource rather than being split into population, manpower, troops, army, military power, etc. unless later gameplay demonstrates a concrete need.

---

## 2. Global population model accepted for V1 direction

Population does **not** need to be physically resident cell-by-cell for V1.

The accepted direction is a global-population allocation model:

```text
Faction total population
        ↓
controller desired allocations
        ↓
fronts / expansion / reserve / other accepted roles
        ↓
actual allocation changes over time rather than teleporting instantly
```

A faction with 100,000 population therefore owns one global population pool. The spatial warfare system determines how much of that population is currently committed/effective at each relevant front or operation.

This deliberately chooses a simpler model over a simulation where every region/cell stores its own resident population.

---

## 3. Desired allocation vs actual allocation

Open Fufu should distinguish between:

- **desired commitment** — what the controller currently wants;
- **actual commitment** — what the simulation has currently managed to distribute/redeploy.

Changing controller policy should not teleport population instantly between distant fronts.

Conceptually:

```text
Desired:
Front A  50%
Front B  20%
Reserve  30%

Actual now:
Front A  30%
Front B  40%
Reserve  30%

Over time:
Actual -> Desired
```

The exact redeployment/inertia rules remain unresolved and should be designed only after the front/sector allocation abstraction is settled.

Future factors may include distance, terrain, railways, connectivity, structures, or items, but none of those modifiers is yet locked.

---

## 4. Allocation API must have a low floor and high ceiling

A four-direction (`north/east/south/west`) allocation model is rejected because faction geometry can be arbitrarily shaped and disconnected.

Raw cell-by-cell allocation must also not be the only practical interface. Requiring ordinary users to identify and assign thousands of individual border cells would make the programming game unnecessarily inaccessible and disproportionately reward mathematical/computational-geometry expertise.

The eventual controller surface must support both:

- **low-floor strategy** — useful high-level commands that can competently distribute population without requiring geometry expertise;
- **high-ceiling strategy** — progressively deeper control for advanced users who want to optimize fronts, sectors, objectives, corridors, or spatial weights.

Current promising hierarchy, not yet fully locked:

```text
target/opponent-level strategy
        ↓
derived front allocation
        ↓
front operations/objectives
        ↓
automatically derived sectors
        ↓
advanced custom spatial weighting
        ↓
engine-level cell distribution
```

The engine should perform the low-level geometric bookkeeping. The player should primarily express strategic intent.

---

## 5. FFY is event-driven income

**FFY (Fufu Yen)** remains the single primary economic currency.

FFY should be generated by explicit economic/earning events rather than a generic passive formula such as `population -> FFY per second`.

Examples of FFY-producing events include:

- successful trade-ship completion;
- train/station economic events;
- capturing specific valuable things or objectives;
- inherited OpenFront economic events that remain appropriate;
- future structures/events deliberately added to the economy.

This makes gold/FFY strategy a problem of maximizing the **frequency and/or value of FFY-producing events**.

Population commitment to warfare does not inherently disable or reduce FFY generation. War affects the economy through the actual events and consequences it changes rather than through a hidden civilian-versus-military labor model.

---

## 6. Warfare as a possible but non-optimal FFY path

Some warfare-related events may generate FFY, for example capturing valuable assets/objectives.

This means an aggressive controller may be able to finance itself partly through conquest, but warfare should not automatically become the optimal pure-economic strategy.

The exact earning events and values remain balance data.

---

## 7. Trade strategy should support frequency-versus-value tradeoffs

Economic infrastructure should permit genuine strategic choices between many lower-value events and fewer higher-value events.

Illustrative example only:

```text
Level 1 dock:
    lower revenue per successful trade
    more total docks/ships may be affordable

Level 3 dock:
    substantially higher revenue per successful trade
    fewer total trade routes may exist
```

In safe conditions, higher-level infrastructure may dominate on raw efficiency. Under hostile naval pressure, multiple lower-level routes may provide diversification: losing one ship does not eliminate the entire income stream.

The point is not the exact `3.5x` example discussed during design; it is the accepted goal that controllers can optimize **event frequency, event value, and risk exposure** rather than following one universally correct FFY build.

---

## 8. War state is derived from hostilities

FFA factions remain attackable opponents and cannot form formal alliances mid-match.

A separate symmetric `atWar`-style state should describe whether two factions are currently engaged in active hostilities.

Conceptually:

```text
Fufu performs hostile action against Tanya
        ↓
Fufu <-> Tanya atWar = true
```

If neither faction performs relevant hostile actions for a sufficiently long rules-defined period, the war state may return to false.

`atWar` does **not** determine whether an FFA opponent is legally attackable. It describes current hostilities and can be used by controllers, UI, trade penalties, and similar systems.

This permits emergent ceasefires and emergent coalition-like targeting without introducing formal in-match diplomacy.

Exact hostile-action definitions and the inactivity timeout remain unresolved.

---

## 9. Trade remains possible during war

Trade with enemies remains allowed.

Being at war should reduce the profitability/effectiveness of trade with that faction rather than make trade impossible. The previously discussed approximate 50%–67% wartime penalty remains a candidate balance range, not a locked number.

This keeps trade-centric FFY strategies viable in FFA while still making prolonged hostilities economically meaningful.

---

## 10. Current accepted world ontology

The current design direction can be summarized as:

```text
MATCH
│
├── Factions
│   ├── Population
│   ├── FFY
│   ├── Territory
│   ├── Structures / units
│   ├── Team
│   ├── Loadout
│   └── Controller
│
├── Map
│   └── Cells
│       ├── owner
│       ├── terrain
│       └── structures / spatial effects
│
├── Derived fronts
│   ├── opponent
│   ├── geometry
│   ├── sectors (candidate derived abstraction)
│   ├── actual commitment
│   └── desired commitment
│
└── Operations
    ├── hold
    ├── push
    ├── push toward objective
    ├── withdraw
    └── advanced/custom spatial weighting
```

The exact front/sector API is still being designed.

---

## 11. Still unresolved in this subsystem

The next mechanics discussion should settle, or at least narrow:

1. how derived fronts are identified and kept stable as territory changes;
2. whether/how fronts are automatically subdivided into sectors;
3. how a beginner allocates population without manually normalizing many percentages;
4. how multiple simultaneous operations on one front divide its commitment;
5. how desired commitment transitions into actual commitment over time;
6. whether a global reserve is explicit and how it behaves;
7. how neutral-territory expansion consumes/uses population relative to hostile fronts;
8. what happens when requested commitments exceed 100% of total population;
9. how casualties are attributed to local commitments and returned to the global population total;
10. which terrain/rail/structure effects influence redeployment in V1;
11. exact `atWar` hostile-action and timeout semantics;
12. exact wartime trade penalty.

Supply/connectivity remains deliberately deferred until these population-allocation mechanics are clear.
